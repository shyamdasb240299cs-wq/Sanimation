## 1. CORE VISION

The audience should feel like **they themselves** wake up as Spider-Man. The video starts as exhilarating wish fulfillment, becomes a slapstick disaster, then pivots to an emotional understanding of responsibility. It should look like a professionally art-directed, animated short, not a slideshow of static pictures.

**Story rhythm:**
1. **0–23s: Hilarious shock and disaster** — upside-down awakening, triumphant rooftop jump, horrified midair discovery, non-graphic crash.
2. **23–46s: Superpower spectacle and humiliating comedy** — abilities, trial-and-error swinging, empty webs, chemistry joke, walking and GPS joke.
3. **46–65s: Fame becomes a burden** — screaming fans and flashbulbs transition into claustrophobic isolation and quiet loneliness.
4. **65–81.4s: Choice of a real hero** — a vulnerable child needs help; he chooses to act without any applause. Genuine, understated emotion.
5. **81.4–86.8s: Wordless playful CTA** — little red-blue spider descends from a ceiling and webs the lens to reveal “LIKE & SUBSCRIBE”.

### Strong creative rules
- The first image must be visually confusing **on purpose**: he seems to be lying in bed, then camera rotates to reveal he is *on the ceiling* and the bed is far below.
- The 50-story height must be visually understandable: roof edge, huge canyon of buildings, cars as tiny dots far below.
- He must **NOT have wrist-mounted web-shooters during the first jump**. Mechanical shooters appear only when he retrieves and straps them on for the training montage. He runs out of *fluid*, not hardware, later.
- Play the fall for humor, not violence. A dust cloud and harmless impact crater are enough; no bones, blood, injuries or gore.
- Show actual traversal and movement (crawling, swinging, running) using moving cameras and animated poses, not static character changes.
- Pivot from fast-paced cuts to slower, stable shots after “But slowly, the excitement disappears.”
- The ending child rescue must look achievable through agility/speed even without web fluid. He uses his body and attention, not magical newly restored webs.
- “No cameras, no applause” is shown on a nearly empty side street, away from the earlier crowds.
- Do **not** narrate the final CTA. The audience sees the spider descend, shoot a web at the lens, and the text appears.

## 2. VISUAL STYLE AND PRODUCTION TECHNIQUE

**Art direction:** premium warm stylized 3D with painterly textures and controlled depth of field; character animation slightly exaggerated like a high-end animated comedy. Cinematic composition and parallax, not hard photorealism, not low-effort AI stock imagery. Deep red, dark blue, charcoal buildings, warm yellow taxis, cool blue shadows. Materials: fabric with subtle embossed web texture, brushed silver web-shooter hardware, reflective glass, warm brick, concrete, glossy street paint. Character silhouettes must remain readable on mobile.

**Motion language:** 3D orbit/dolly for character moments, fast tracked fly-through for swing, tasteful whip-pan for midair reveal, subtle handheld instability only in comedy/fame scenes, locked steady camera in emotional ending. Add controlled motion blur, lens refraction and realistic depth-of-field selectively, no heavy bloom that hides action.

**Lighting timeline:**
- Bedroom: gentle 8 a.m. golden sunrise, soft window streaks.
- Rooftop + first jump: sunny late morning, bright atmospheric skyline.
- Superpower and swing montage: noon transitioning to warm afternoon.
- Walking + crowds: late afternoon city warmth, shop signs coming alive.
- Reflection + rescue: muted blue-hour dusk. Keep enough ambient light to read gestures.
- CTA: softly lit dark bedroom interior with a white ceiling and isolated tiny spider.

**9:16 composition:** Always preview at mobile size. Put protagonist within central 65% of frame during crucial actions; keep top and bottom 15% safe for platform UI/captions. Verticality is the signature: tall buildings extend beyond frame, ground vanishes below, swings rise toward skyscrapers, ceiling reveal exploits phone portrait format.

**Efficient creation:** Reuse one bedroom, one modular rooftop, 4–6 reusable building facades, one modular Manhattan street, one quiet side street. Camera movement, crowd variants, lighting, and parallax provide variety. Use Eevee/appropriate real-time Blender renderer when performance matters; render layered passes for heavy city/crowd scenes if necessary. Do not create unique fully detailed interiors for every shot.

## 3. CHARACTER BIBLE / MODEL CONTINUITY

### A. Main hero (same model throughout)
- Young adult, athletic/slender superhero body, not massive bodybuilder; flexible shoulders and limbs.
- Full red-and-deep-blue fabric suit, stylized web pattern on red panels, clear large white eye lenses with animated expressive squints; face remains masked except optional very brief glimpses of wide eyes through mask animation. Do not switch to a different hero/face in mid-video.
- Suit design should evoke a modern web-slinging superhero with agile silhouette, but avoid reproducing an identifiable film actor’s likeness from photos.
- At awakening and first rooftop jump: **NO SHOOTERS ON WRISTS**. Show unmistakably bare fabric wrists during desperate wrist motions.
- In short retrieval montage: finds two small silver wrist-mounted mechanical launchers back in his room, clips them securely on. In all subsequent swinging and walking shots, shooters stay visible.
- Body language arc: goofy/excited → confident → embarrassed → overstimulated → weary and inward-looking → calm/decisive.
- Do not change suit color, logos, head shape, or eye-lens design between shots.

### B. Child (end sequence)
- 6–8-year-old, small frame, yellow rain jacket, teal backpack, blue shoes; model easy to recognize in one second.
- Child steps carelessly toward a side-street crossing; a delivery bike or compact car approaches from a blind corner. Hero notices and swiftly pulls child to a safe sidewalk **without violence or impact**.
- Show relief and a brief grateful exchange via body language, not spoken dialogue.

### C. New York crowd
- 8–15 modular low-poly/stylized varied adult civilians, repeated with varied colors, hair, hats, bags and animation timing to appear larger.
- Phones face toward hero; flashes occur during fan frenzy. Keep facial detail simple but expressions legible.
- One confused pedestrian notices hero walking instead of swinging; one excited fan leans toward him for a selfie.

### D. Tiny CTA spider
- Entirely separate adorable stylized spider design, body 75% red / 25% blue with tiny white eyes and eight readable short legs; glossy/cartoonish, not frightening.
- Descends from an ordinary indoor ceiling on a single thread. Turns to camera, raises tiny forelegs, fires a web directly into the lens; web expands into readable “LIKE & SUBSCRIBE”. No spoken words.

### E. Background props
- Smartphone with a **generic navigation interface**; use a map-pin route and comic footstep icon. If using Google Maps by name in captions, do not copy its complete interface or logo.
- Two mechanical wrist devices; fluid cartridges visibly run empty; no refill after depletion.
- Yellow taxis, green street signs, rooftop water towers, brick tenements, fire escapes, glass skyscrapers, concrete parapets, skyline silhouettes, street crosswalk, a safety awning/dumpster for non-graphic crash.

## 4. ENVIRONMENT / CITY SET DESIGN

### BEDROOM SET (morning + tiny spider outro)
- Small modern NYC apartment bedroom: pale off-white ceiling, charcoal-blue accent wall, low bed with rumpled gray blanket and yellow pillow, side table with phone, window framing a fire escape and warm city daylight, one poster and desk lamp.
- **Geometry:** actual bed-to-ceiling height readable from wide shot; camera rotation reveal is physically coherent. The ceiling should visibly support his inverted hands/feet.
- For final CTA, use same room for visual bookending, but no human visible. Camera looks straight up toward ceiling corner, shallow depth of field.

### FIFTY-STORY ROOFTOP
- Flat top of tall midtown office building: concrete roof, metal railing/parapet, HVAC units, satellite dish, distant rooftop water towers. Main edge faces a long north-south Manhattan-style avenue.
- **Orientation:** hero jumps from avenue-facing edge; street below visible as narrow strip with yellow taxi dots. Establish one orientation so fall, wall scrape and impact feel spatially continuous.

### CITY WIDE VIEW
- NYC-inspired canyon of buildings: alternating brick/brownstone midrises, glass office towers, iconic distant skyline silhouettes, yellow taxis and lane markings, fire escapes, food trucks, store awnings and green street signs. A bridge or river visible in at least one aerial shot for immediate New York recognition.
- Never place floating disconnected buildings. Streets connect in an orthogonal grid, sidewalks line both sides, intersections are readable. No unrealistic giant billboards covering entire sky.

### ROOFTOP TRAINING + SWING CORRIDOR
- Start on same rooftop, then use several adjacent buildings of different heights; design clear anchor points. Swings are pendulum arcs: webs taut to fixed high attachment points on facades, body follows an arc before release, next line shoots to next building. Camera tracks with hero, maintaining a clear sense of forward progress.

### CROWD STREET
- Sidewalk at intersection with crosswalk, storefront awnings, bicycle rack, parked taxis. Make it wide enough for rising crowd, then subtly compress space with lenses/camera blocking so fame feels overwhelming.

### QUIET SIDE STREET / RESCUE
- A few blocks from crowds: 2–3-story brick facade with fire escape, narrow road, pedestrian crossing, parked delivery van partly blocking visibility, bicycle approaching at safe controllable speed, streetlamp turning on at dusk. Open sidewalk space provides safe place to pull child out of path.

## 6. CAMERA AND EDITING GRAMMAR

- **Opening:** counterintuitive inverted shot → 180° roll reveal → hero push-in. Must be readable in first 3 seconds.
- **Roof sequence:** alternate huge vertical scale (50-story wide) and tiny facial/hand gags (empty wrists). Do not reuse the same angle through the whole fall.
- **Swing sequence:** wide establishing + over-shoulder flying camera + front 3/4 close-up. Web line anchors to building, not the sky. Pendulum movement must match physics enough to feel convincing.
- **Walk gag:** fixed distant camera and long beat is funnier than more frantic edits; walking vs flying is the contrast.
- **Crowds:** camera inches uncomfortably close, phones at frame edges, very quick cuts early in fame. Slow down from “excitement disappears.”
- **Rooftop reflection:** one beautiful 3-second quiet composition with the hero small against an immense city; avoid generic character looking at horizon in every emotional cut.
- **Rescue:** clearly establish hazard, child, hero, and available path before cut. Use a wide shot to demonstrate the actual rescue (not a cut that hides it).
- **CTA:** precise lens web-splat animation; text is motion-graphics overlay and remains legible.

**Captions:** optional but recommended. Dynamically caption spoken words in clean bold sans-serif, white with narrow dark stroke and selective yellow emphasis for “CEILING”, “JUMP”, “WEB-SHOOTERS”, “WALKING”, and the emotional final line. Don't cover faces, wrists, child or CTA. Always use the actual transcript.
